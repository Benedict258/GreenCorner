Solar phone charger

Charge a phone from the sun. You build the whole thing in one afternoon and learn how solar power is stored and used.

What you'll build

A small solar charger that stores the sun's energy in two rechargeable cells and gives it back through a USB port, so you can top up a phone or a power bank.

You will wire the panel to a charging module, fit the cells, add a switch and a boost module, then test it in real sunlight.

Everything you need is in the kit. You only need a screwdriver and, if you want neat joints, a soldering iron.

What you'll learn

  • How a solar panel turns sunlight into electricity
  • How to charge and protect a Li-ion cell safely
  • What voltage and current are, and how to measure them
  • How to read a simple wiring diagram
  • Why solar systems store energy in batteries

Step-by-step guide

  1. 1Test the solar panel
    Photo · multimeter on the panel leads · 3:2

    Put the panel in direct sunlight. Set a multimeter to DC volts and touch the probes to the red and black wires. You should read about 6V. If you read much less, tilt the panel towards the sun.

  2. 2Fit the cells in the holder
    Photo · two cells in the holder · 3:2

    Look for the + and − marks on the holder. Push each 18650 cell in with the flat end on the spring. Never force a cell in the wrong way round.

  3. 3Connect the charger module
    Diagram · wiring to the charger module · 3:2

    Join the panel wires to the IN+ and IN− pads of the TP4056 module, and the battery holder to B+ and B−. Check every joint before moving on.

  4. 4Add the switch and boost module
    Photo · boost module and switch · 3:2

    Wire the switch between the module output and the boost module input. The boost module turns the cell voltage into steady 5V for the USB port.

  5. 5Test it and charge a phone
    Photo · phone charging · 3:2

    Leave the panel in the sun until the module's light turns blue. Switch on, plug in your phone and watch it charge.

Safety notes

  • Li-ion cells can get hot if their terminals touch or if they are put in backwards. Check polarity every time.
  • Charge cells only through the charger module.
  • Soldering irons are hot. Ask an adult to supervise.
